Current Position

Postdoctoral Researcher · Technische Universität Berlin

My current research focuses on the application of process-based modelling to investigate hydrological processes, groundwater dynamics, ecosystem responses, and climate-resilient land management under changing environmental conditions. Building on my previous work on wet grasslands, peatlands, and groundwater-driven ecosystem dynamics, I study how managed landscapes respond to shifts in climate and disturbance regimes associated with the “new normal,” including changing precipitation patterns, water availability, and extreme events.

NOMOS

Ecohydrology and Water Resources Management Group · Technische Universität Berlin

+
Current project

New climate extremes and the emergence of a “new normal”

NOMOS investigates how climatic and hydrological extremes that were previously considered exceptional may increasingly become recurring or characteristic features of changing climate regimes.

My work focuses on the interdisciplinary conceptualisation of the “new normal,” including how it is defined, detected, and interpreted across different scientific fields. I examine the transition from historical variability to emerging extreme conditions and consider the implications for hydrological systems, ecosystems, infrastructure, and long-term adaptation.

Soil knowledge, long-term experiments, and impact assessment

SoilWise supports the development of accessible and harmonised soil knowledge for research, policy, and sustainable land management.

ICaW2

Data and Simulation Research Group · Leibniz Centre for Agricultural Landscape Research (ZALF), Germany

+
Doctoral research project

Modelling wet-grassland productivity and biogeochemical fluxes

Within ICaW2, I investigated how groundwater conditions, climate, vegetation dynamics, and management affect wet-grassland productivity and biogeochemical processes.

Using process-based agroecosystem modelling, environmental observations, and climate scenarios, I evaluated biomass production as well as water, carbon, and nitrogen fluxes under contrasting groundwater regimes. The project formed the principal scientific framework of my doctoral research.

LysiWater

Hydrology Research Group · Agricultural Research, Education and Extension Organization (AREEO), Iran

+
Agricultural water research

Lysimeter-based assessment of crop water requirements

LysiWater focused on the measurement and evaluation of crop water requirements under semi-arid environmental conditions using lysimeter experiments.

My research contributed to estimating evapotranspiration, irrigation demand, and crop coefficients. This work established the experimental and hydrological foundation for my later research on soil–water–plant interactions and process-based environmental modelling.
